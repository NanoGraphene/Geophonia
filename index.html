Understand what you hear

Higher temperature colors produce higher notes. A greater proportion of colored trace pixels produces a louder tone. No qualifying trace produces two low notes, then silence.

Source, projection & scientific limits

Global Sea Surface Currents and Temperature uses the ECCO2 ocean model. Frame 23000 corresponds to March 25, 2007, 12:00 UTC, per NASA’s frame-date table. The model’s averaging interval is unverified.

The original 2048 × 1024 RGB frame has no embedded CRS. Full-image equirectangular alignment was checked against Natural Earth land geometry: 99.44% land/ocean agreement away from coarse coastline boundaries and eight checkpoint matches. This is empirical alignment, not authoritative georeferencing. A derived Web Mercator image uses nearest-neighbor resampling with a ±85.0511° latitude limit. Geographic names and surface announcements are withheld.

Credit: NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio. Visualization contributors: Greg Shirah, Horace Mitchell, Eric Sokolowsky; scientists Hong Zhang and Dimitris Menemenlis. Independent challenge submission; no NASA endorsement. NASA media usage guidelines. Alignment reference: Natural Earth public-domain 1:110m land geometry.

Sonification uses the original source colorbar, sampled from its interior. Pixels need brightness ≥24/255, chroma ≥20/255, saturation ≥0.3, and a chromatic RGB distance ≤0.2 (subtract minimum channel, divide by chroma) from that palette. The mean matched palette position sets pitch exponentially across your chosen range; qualifying-pixel proportion sets amplitude linearly. These are separate visual features, with fixed thresholds across the source. Gray land, black ocean gaps, and unmatched colors are not scientific zero or a verified no-data flag.

Pointer exit fades the tone; keyboard exploration continues at the selected location while using controls. Refocusing the map or moving resumes exploration sound. Leaving the browser tab silences all audio and requires an explicit restart. No speech, timeline, geographic surface cues, or automatic playback is included in milestones 2–3.
