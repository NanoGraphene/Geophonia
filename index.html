Brief shoreline notifications

Entering land: a short, low wooden knock. Entering water: a brighter droplet. The data tone stays neutral on both surfaces. Near-shore jitter is suppressed.

Map edges & corners

Soft, filtered mallet sounds mark the edges. Left: one low stroke. Right: three higher strokes. Top rises; bottom falls. Corners combine vertical, then horizontal.

These are the visible interactive map boundaries, not the physical edges of your screen. Shorelines are approximate: small islands and narrow channels may be omitted; crossing notifications wait until the cursor clears a small uncertain shoreline zone. Country boundaries never change the surface sound.

A LITTLE ORIENTATION

Explore at your own pace.

Move across Earth

Focus the map and use arrow keys, move your pointer, or enter coordinates. The starting point is 30° N, 45° W.

Understand what you hear

Higher temperature colors produce higher notes. A greater proportion of colored trace pixels produces a louder tone. No qualifying trace means no data tone, without repeated beeps. Land/water crossings have brief notification sounds, independent of data availability.

Global Sea Surface Currents and Temperature uses the ECCO2 ocean model. Frame 23000 corresponds to March 25, 2007, 12:00 UTC, per NASA’s frame-date table. The model’s averaging interval is unverified.

For the ocean source, the original 2048 × 1024 RGB frame has no embedded CRS. Full-image equirectangular alignment was checked against Natural Earth land geometry: 99.44% land/ocean agreement away from coarse coastline boundaries and eight checkpoint matches. This is empirical alignment, not authoritative georeferencing. A derived Web Mercator image uses nearest-neighbor resampling with a ±85.0511° latitude limit. Surface classification uses a separate Natural Earth 1:50m land/lake mask. Its empirical alignment agrees with the rendered land at 99.73% of tested pixels away from coasts; four coastline transects agree within one source pixel. Surface cues remain approximate, and geographic names are withheld.

Ocean visualization credits: NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio. Visualization contributors: Greg Shirah, Horace Mitchell, Eric Sokolowsky; scientists Hong Zhang and Dimitris Menemenlis. Independent challenge submission; no NASA endorsement. NASA media usage guidelines. Alignment reference: Natural Earth public-domain 1:110m land geometry. Surface classification: public-domain 1:50m land and lakes, rasterized to 4096 × 2048 cells (about 9.8 km at the equator), with polygon holes preserved. Lakes count as water; islands within lakes retain the underlying land mask. Current generalized geometry is not a historical 2007 shoreline. Geographic sources and resolution.

Ocean traces use the source colorbar; values are image-derived.

Pointer exit fades the tone; keyboard exploration continues at the selected location while using controls. Refocusing the map or moving resumes exploration sound. Leaving the browser tab silences all audio and requires an explicit restart. A neutral harmonic synth carries data pitch and coverage, identically over land and water. Short notifications identify shoreline crossings: a 45 ms low knock entering land and an 85 ms higher droplet entering water. A 3 × 3 geographic-cell hysteresis zone suppresses jitter. At most two recent crossings play per movement; no growing queue. Coordinate jumps and resets establish a new baseline without crossing sounds. No background surface guide or geography-dependent filter remains. Speech, timeline and automatic playback remain future milestones.
