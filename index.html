    04 / FIND YOUR WAY

    Hear the shape of the map.

    Reference examples pause exploration. All sounds work in mono. Move or refocus the map to return.

    Brief shoreline notifications

    Entering land: a short, low wooden knock. Entering water: a brighter droplet. The data tone stays neutral on both surfaces. Near-shore jitter is suppressed.

    Map edges & corners

    One identical drum note marks every edge and corner; HRTF placement identifies where it came from.

    These are the visible interactive map boundaries, not the physical edges of your screen. Shorelines are approximate: small islands and narrow channels may be omitted; crossing notifications wait until the cursor clears a small uncertain shoreline zone. Country boundaries never change the surface sound.

    A LITTLE ORIENTATION

    Explore at your own pace.

    Move across Earth

    Focus the map and use arrow keys, move your pointer, or enter coordinates. The starting point is 30° N, 45° W.

    Understand what you hear

    Higher temperature colors produce higher notes. A greater proportion of colored trace pixels produces a louder tone. No qualifying trace means no data tone, without repeated beeps. Land/water crossings have brief notification sounds, independent of data availability.

    Source, projection & scientific limits

    Full-resolution NASA rendered frame · image-derived values

    Open original NASA frame ↗ · Verified temperature-frame provenance (JSON)

    Global Sea Surface Currents and Temperature uses the ECCO2 ocean model. Frame 23000 corresponds to March 25, 2007, 12:00 UTC, per NASA’s frame-date table. The model’s averaging interval is unverified.

    For the ocean source, the original 2048 × 1024 RGB frame has no embedded CRS. Full-image equirectangular alignment was checked against Natural Earth land geometry: 99.44% land/ocean agreement away from coarse coastline boundaries and eight checkpoint matches. This is empirical alignment, not authoritative georeferencing. A derived Web Mercator image uses nearest-neighbor resampling with a ±85.0511° latitude limit. Surface classification uses a separate Natural Earth 1:50m land/lake mask. Its empirical alignment agrees with the rendered land at 99.73% of tested pixels away from coasts; four coastline transects agree within one source pixel. Surface cues remain approximate, and place names use separate approximate Natural Earth country, marine and lake polygons.

Lakes count as water; islands within lakes retain the underlying land mask. Current generalized geometry is not a historical 2007 shoreline. Geographic sources and resolution.

    Ocean traces use the source colorbar; values are image-derived.

    Pointer exit fades the tone; keyboard exploration continues at the selected location while using controls. Refocusing the map or moving resumes exploration sound. Leaving the browser tab silences all audio and requires an explicit restart. A neutral harmonic synth carries data pitch and coverage, identically over land and water. Short notifications identify shoreline crossings: a 45 ms low knock entering land and an 85 ms higher droplet entering water. A 3 × 3 geographic-cell hysteresis zone suppresses jitter. At most two recent crossings play per movement; no growing queue. Coordinate jumps and resets establish a new baseline without crossing sounds. No background surface guide or geography-dependent filter remains. Instant browser speech reads the selected place, estimated temperature and date. Left/Right steps through the available frames and periods. Automatic playback remains a future milestone.
